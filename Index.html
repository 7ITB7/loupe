Identify one stamp

One stamp or cover, shot well, ideally with your perforation gauge and a known color in the same frame.

How to shoot it
  • Daylight beside a window, no flash, no shadow across the stamp.
  • Phone parallel to the table. Tap to focus on the stamp.
  • Fill the frame. Claude sees each photo at roughly 1.2 megapixels, so a stamp that fills a quarter of the frame is only a few hundred pixels wide. Crop in before you send.
  • Gauge touching the stamp's edge, flat in the same plane. Color reference right next to it.
  • Backlit: hold the stamp against a bright, even screen.

If you measured with the real gauge, that overrides what the photo suggests.

The sharpest reading takes a minute or two. Value is an estimate; the sold-listing buttons are the check.

Rare check: 1¢ and 2¢

Franklin 1¢ and Washington 2¢ stamps are mostly worth cents. A short list is worth hundreds to many thousands. Answer what you can see; the list narrows to what is still possible and tells you the test for each.

What this can and can't do. It can rule varieties out and tell you exactly what to measure. It cannot prove a stamp genuine, and neither can a photo. A stamp that survives every test goes to an expert for a certificate (Philatelic Foundation, PSE or APS) before you sell it as anything rare.

Which stamp is it?
Edges
Faint grid of tiny dots or ridges pressed into the paper (tilt it in the light)
Perforation gauge
Watermark (held up to a bright light or on a dark tray)

Tap an answer again to clear it. Anything you leave blank is treated as unknown.
